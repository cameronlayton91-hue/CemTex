READ THIS RIGHT BEFORE THE INTERVIEW

Five-Minute Cheat Sheet

How I Lead

High autonomy + high accountability. Clear expectations. Inspect the business without hovering over good people. Coach aggressively and give strong performers room to run.

How I Grow

Protect the existing base, then identify the growth pools: share of wallet, product penetration, geography, lost business, strategic distributors and cross-brand opportunities.

Performance

Revenue is a lagging indicator. Watch pipeline creation, conversion, margin, forecast accuracy and strategic-account progress.

Underperformance

Diagnose whether the issue is capability, effort, strategy or market. Coach it, set the plan, follow up, and make the hard decision if the performance does not change.

Executive Thinking

Profitable growth, customer concentration, talent, forecast credibility, cross-functional execution and a commercial process that scales.

Role Scale

South is roughly a $25M business and Texcel's largest region. Treat it like stewardship of a business, not supervision of a rep count.

Why Me

Growth experience + industrial/distributor selling + leadership + commercial discipline + ability to think across brands and channels.

Desired CEO Reaction

“He can run this organization. He will tell me the truth, develop the team, create structure without bureaucracy and grow the business.”

YOUR DIFFERENTIATOR

Positioning Lines

If you're looking for someone who can think strategically across several brands, connect overlapping channels, integrate teams and help build a broader commercial platform, that's where I believe I separate myself.”

Q&A

Leadership & Management

How would you describe your leadership style?
I give people a lot of ownership, but ownership only works when expectations are clear. I want salespeople to feel like they're running their own businesses. They should know their customers, pipeline, priorities and number.

My role is to set direction, remove obstacles, coach and hold them accountable. I inspect the business through pipeline, forecast, customer activity and results. If those are healthy, strong people get room to execute.
Land on: high autonomy + high accountability.
How do you hold salespeople accountable?
Accountability starts with clarity. I want the person to understand the revenue target, but also the leading indicators that create the result.

If somebody misses, I diagnose the reason first, agree on an action plan, establish a follow-up cadence and then hold them to it. I'm supportive, but the salesperson ultimately owns the result.
How do you lead experienced reps who know more product than you?
I would expect experienced people to know their customers, territories and product details better than I do on day one.

I'm not there to prove I'm smarter than the technical experts. My value is helping them prioritize, challenge assumptions, identify untapped opportunities, improve deal strategy and use company resources better.
How do you build trust with a new team?
Consistency and follow-through. Early on I listen, travel with the team, understand their customers and frustrations, and identify a few problems I can actually help solve.

People trust leadership faster when they see that you listen, make good decisions and do what you said you would do.
What do you do with an underperforming salesperson?
First determine whether it's a capability problem, effort problem, strategy problem or market problem.

Capability gets coaching. Strategy gets changed. Market or resource issues get addressed. If the problem is effort or accountability, expectations get very clear very quickly.

People deserve coaching and clarity, but I'm comfortable making the personnel decision when somebody repeatedly fails to execute after receiving support.
Diagnose first. Act decisively second.
How do you handle a top performer with a difficult personality?
Performance buys credibility, but it doesn't buy permission to damage the culture.

I address the behavior privately and directly. If the behavior is hurting collaboration, customers or the team, it has to change. Top performers should raise the organization's standard, not create a separate rulebook for themselves.
Q&A

Growth & Strategy

How do you drive growth through a sales team?
First determine where growth can actually come from: existing customers, product penetration, geography, market potential, lost business and pipeline quality.

Then turn that into a small number of specific priorities for each salesperson. After that, execution needs a cadence: what's moving, what's stuck, what changed, what support is needed and what happens next?
How would you assess the organization in your first 90 days?
Days 1–30: Learn the people, customers, products, economics and operating rhythm. Spend time in the field and analyze revenue, margin, concentration, pipeline and forecast.

Days 31–60: Identify the highest-value growth opportunities, coverage gaps and talent issues.

Days 61–90: Establish the priorities, scorecard and operating cadence required to execute.
“I want my first changes to be based on evidence, not ego.”
How do you grow existing accounts?
Existing accounts are growth platforms, not just maintenance accounts.

I want to understand our share of wallet, what product categories they buy from us, what they buy elsewhere, who influences the purchase decision and where we're vulnerable.
How do you win against an entrenched competitor?
Find the wedge. It could be availability, service, lead time, technical support, pricing structure, ease of doing business or a product gap.

Win something specific, execute extremely well and expand from there. Share of wallet is often won category by category.
How do you balance growth and margin?
The goal is profitable, durable growth.

Revenue that destroys margin or creates operational problems isn't automatically good growth. I want to understand pricing power, cost-to-serve and which opportunities justify strategic investment.
Example: $30M at 60% gross margin = $18M GP. $50M at 40% = $20M GP. Twenty million more sales only created two million more gross profit.
IMPORTANT ANSWER

Three-Year Plan

If you stepped into the South role, what's your three-year plan?

I would start by treating the roughly $25M South business as something I have to protect before I try to redesign it.

In the first six to nine months, I'd segment revenue by customer, product family, margin and salesperson; travel with the team; meet our largest distributors; assess pipeline quality and coverage; and identify where we're underpenetrated.

By the end of year one, I want a credible forecast, clear territory and account plans, stronger product-penetration data and a short list of growth priorities.

In year two, I would double down on two or three repeatable plays — cross-selling sister brands, targeted competitor conversions and selected verticals like data-center cooling.

In year three, I want to scale what worked, develop leaders underneath me and make South a model for profitable multi-brand growth that can be replicated elsewhere.
Q&A

CRM, Forecasting & Operating Rhythm

What's your view of CRM?
CRM is valuable when it helps the salesperson and management run the business. It becomes useless when it's treated like a data-entry punishment.

My standard is simple: meaningful opportunities are current, next steps are clear, close dates are realistic and the pipeline reflects reality.

If leadership actually uses the information to help people win and make decisions, adoption gets much easier.
How do you forecast?
Build the forecast from opportunity-level evidence, not by asking salespeople how they feel about the month.

I look at stage, customer commitment, timing, historical conversion, next action and known risk. Large opportunities need to be challenged because one delayed project can distort an entire forecast.
What KPIs would you manage?
Revenue, gross margin, growth versus plan, pipeline creation, pipeline coverage, conversion, forecast accuracy, strategic-account progress and new-category wins.

I want enough activity data to diagnose problems, but I don't want people rewarded for logging meaningless activity.
How often do you review pipeline?
Weekly visibility into major opportunities and forecast movement, with deeper territory/account reviews monthly or quarterly.

The objective isn't interrogation. It's making sure priorities are clear, risk gets surfaced early and leadership resources are applied to the right deals.
Q&A

CEO-Level Thinking

How would you communicate with me as CEO?
Keep you informed without making you manage the sales organization for me.

I'd establish a predictable cadence around performance, forecast, major wins, major risks, talent and anything requiring executive involvement.

If I bring you a problem, I want to bring you my recommendation too. Bad news should travel fast.
How do you create enterprise value, not just sales?
Improve the quality of growth: customer diversification, product penetration, distributor relationships, repeatable process, credible forecasting, stronger talent and healthier margins.

A business becomes more valuable when growth doesn't depend on one rainmaker or a handful of customers.
Why are you ready for a larger leadership role now?
Because my responsibilities have already expanded beyond a traditional territory role.

I've spent years developing business, training and supporting other salespeople, helping on larger opportunities and thinking beyond one account list.

The next step I want is formal ownership of the people, process and strategy — where success is measured by what the organization produces.
How do I know you can manage instead of just sell?
I don't view management as jumping into every opportunity and closing it for the salesperson.

I've already had to influence, train, support and drive execution through other people, including situations where I didn't have formal authority.

What excites me about the role is building the system around the team: priorities, coaching, pipeline discipline, account strategy and accountability.
Why should I hire you?
Because I bring both sides of what the role needs.

I have a proven commercial background and understand how to win customers, but I'm also thinking about how to make an entire sales organization perform.

I understand distributors, complex industrial selling, major-account development and operating across territories. I'm comfortable being accountable for a number, developing people and having difficult conversations.

I'm looking for somewhere I can build something, not just make a lateral move.
You don't have the same hose depth as some existing leaders. Why you?
I would never pretend I know more hose than people who have spent decades in the industry.

Their technical depth is an asset I would use, not compete with.

My job is to learn aggressively, ask the right questions, put technical experts into the right customer conversations and make the entire team more productive.

What I bring is growth mindset, distributor selling, leadership, commercial discipline and the ability to think across brands and channels.

20–40 SECOND ANSWERS

CEO Lightning Round

Relationship or process?
Both. Relationships create trust; process makes success repeatable and forecastable.
Revenue or margin?
Profitable growth. I don't want the team buying revenue with margin without a deliberate reason.
First sign a territory is in trouble?
Weak pipeline creation before the revenue number actually misses.
First sign a salesperson is in trouble?
Avoidance: stale opportunities, vague next steps, weak customer activity and excuses without a plan.
How much autonomy?
A lot once expectations and guardrails are clear. Autonomy without visibility is abdication.
When do you enter a deal?
When my involvement increases the probability of winning: executive sponsorship, pricing, internal resources, negotiation or strategic importance.
What if your plan is wrong?
Say it early, bring evidence, recommend the better path and change it.
What do you expect from your team?
Preparation, honesty, ownership, customer focus and follow-through.
What should your team expect from you?
Direction, access, coaching, resources, consistency and straight answers.
How do you prioritize?
Economic impact and strategic importance — not whoever is making the most noise.
Missed quarter?
Own it, diagnose the drivers, separate timing from structural issues and act on leading indicators.
Management philosophy?
Give good people ownership, make expectations visible, coach aggressively and hold everyone — including myself — accountable.
WATCH YOUR LANGUAGE

Things Not to Say

Don't: “I'm a hands-off manager.”
Say: high autonomy with visibility and accountability.
Don't: “I treat everyone the same.”
Standards are consistent; coaching adapts to the person.
Don't: “I can sell anything.”
Keep the identity focused on building a team that can win.
Don't: Jump immediately to firing an underperformer.
Diagnose, coach, clarify, then act decisively.
Don't: Pretend the estimated total platform revenue is a known fact.
Let Jake give you the real number.
Don't: Apologize for not being the deepest hose expert.
Respect the experts and position yourself as the commercial leader.
